1. The training

The Eastwest Transpersonal Training School (“the School”, “we”, “us”) provides an educational training in transpersonal psychotherapy, counselling and Holotropic Breathwork®. The training is delivered online to one international cohort, with in-person residential intensives held in Europe, and is structured over approximately four years in three levels:

  • Level 1 — Self-Development (about 1 year): Certificate of Attendance. May be taken on its own as a standalone year of self-development, with no obligation to continue.
  • Level 2 — Counselling Skills (about 1.5 years): Certificate in Transpersonal Counselling Skills, awarded through a EUROTAS-accredited institute, with credits toward EUROTAS certification.
  • Level 3 — Psychotherapy & Breathwork (about 1.5 years): Certificate as Transpersonal Psychotherapy & Holotropic Breathwork Facilitator, with an optional EUROTAS certification track.

The training is based on criteria recognised by the Global Transpersonal Network EUROTAS. The programme includes taught modules, experiential and self-development work, personal therapy, supervision and, at the appropriate levels, supervised practice. The certificate awarded is a “Facilitator” certificate; it is not in itself a licence or the professional title “psychotherapist” (see Section 4).

The School may make reasonable changes to teachers, module order, dates, venues, or the balance of online and in-person delivery, where necessary and without reducing the overall educational value of the training. Where a residential intensive cannot take place as planned, an alternative date, venue or format will be offered.

4. Legal notice — use of the professional title “psychotherapist”

The use of the terms “psychotherapy school,” “psychotherapy training,” and “transpersonal psychotherapy training” in connection with this programme describes the educational content and orientation of the training. These terms do not, by themselves, confer a professional title, licence, registration, accreditation, or legal authorisation to practise as a psychotherapist.

The legal status of psychotherapy and the professional title “psychotherapist” varies considerably between countries. In some jurisdictions, the title is legally protected and may be used only by individuals who meet specific statutory requirements. These requirements may include:

  • a specified university degree or prior professional qualification;
  • completion of a nationally recognised psychotherapy training programme;
  • a prescribed number of clinical practice, supervision, and personal therapy hours;
  • registration with a governmental, statutory, or professional body;
  • professional examinations, licensing, or recognition procedures; and
  • compliance with national healthcare, professional, insurance, and ethical regulations.

Admission to or completion of this programme does not guarantee that a student or graduate will be legally eligible to use the title “psychotherapist,” obtain professional registration, receive reimbursement from healthcare systems or insurers, or practise psychotherapy independently in any particular country.

Each student is responsible for verifying the legal and professional requirements applicable in:

  • their country of residence;
  • every country in which they intend to practise or offer services, including online services; and
  • any country in which their clients may be located, where relevant.

Students should obtain current information directly from the competent governmental authority, statutory regulator, or recognised national professional organisation before enrolling in the programme and again before using a professional title or beginning clinical practice.

The European Association for Psychotherapy provides a directory of National Awarding Organisations that may serve as a starting point for country-specific enquiries:

https://www.europsyche.org/About/National-Representations/National-Awarding-Organisations-NAO

Students should be aware that information provided by professional organisations does not replace national legislation or advice from the relevant regulatory authority. Legal and professional requirements may also change over time.

The programme and its organisers make no representation or guarantee that this training, whether completed in whole or in part, will satisfy the legal, academic, licensing, registration, or professional-recognition requirements of any particular jurisdiction.

5. Code of ethics, conduct and confidentiality

The training involves deep experiential and self-development work, including group process, personal disclosure, and Holotropic Breathwork. To keep the learning environment safe for everyone, the student agrees, throughout the training, to:

  • Confidentiality of others. Keep strictly confidential all personal information, disclosures and experiences shared by fellow students, teachers and clients in the course of the training, and not to record, repeat or publish them.
  • Respect and non-discrimination. Treat all participants, teachers and staff with respect, and refrain from any form of harassment, discrimination or abusive behaviour.
  • Boundaries and safety. Respect appropriate personal, sexual and professional boundaries, and follow the safety guidance given for experiential work and breathwork sessions.
  • Personal responsibility. Take responsibility for their own physical and psychological wellbeing during experiential work, and inform a teacher or facilitator of any relevant health matter (see also the health declaration in Section 8).
  • Supervised practice. When undertaking counselling or supervised clinical practice as part of the training, work only within the limits of their competence and their current level of training, under the supervision arranged or approved by the School, and within an ethical and confidentiality framework consistent with the standards of EUROTAS and, where applicable, the EAP.
  • Honesty about status. Describe themselves to any client or the public honestly and accurately — for example as a student or trainee in supervised practice — and not use any professional title they are not legally entitled to use (see Section 4).
  • No harmful practice. Not offer services, diagnoses or treatments that they are not qualified or authorised to provide, and refer clients on where appropriate.

Serious or repeated breach of this code may lead to suspension or termination of enrolment, following a fair review, without refund of fees for the module in progress.
