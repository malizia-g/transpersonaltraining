### Love as a Spectrum of Consciousness
> Love read developmentally — and the clinical question of how old the client is right now.

Love itself is read as a spectrum of consciousness — from possessive, infantile *ponya* through *eros* and *philia* to transpersonal *agape*, *anoya* and *charis* (grace). Clinically decisive: the therapist asks "how old is the client in front of me right now?" and attunes the intervention to that developmental level, moving the client from love lived as suffering to love lived as offering.

### Maps of the Psyche
*(Jung, Assagioli, Grof, Dressler)*
> The cartographies students learn to navigate: Jung’s Self and shadow, Grof’s perinatal matrices, Dressler’s central drama.

The cartographies students learn to navigate: Jung's model of ego, persona, shadow, complexes, collective unconscious and Self, with individuation as the path of integration; the archetypes as forms without content that organize psychic life; Grof's Basic Perinatal Matrices, linking the stages of biological birth to recurring patterns of experience in non-ordinary states; and Stefan Dressler's map of the central drama — separation and unsafety as the two roots, the inner critic as storyteller and guardian, the five core beliefs, and the victim–perpetrator structure — which gives the one-to-one method "The Central" its theoretical backbone.

### Ancestral Trauma
> Trauma lives at three levels — personal, ancestral and collective — and family constellations make the ancestral one visible.

Trauma is not the event but the nervous system's frozen response to it, and it lives at three levels: personal, ancestral, collective. Family constellations — based on Bert Hellinger's Orders of Love and the *knowing field* — make the ancestral level visible: representatives embody parts of the client's system, revealing entanglements with excluded or forgotten ancestors, and healing sentences and movements restore the natural flow.

Because an ancestral or collective field carries more charge than a single nervous system can process, this work belongs to the group: co-regulation, ritual and a tuned field let frozen material finally move. "Trauma moves when we feel safe." The framework also connects with shamanic and indigenous healing traditions.

### Shadow Work
> What we split off in order to be loved does not disappear; we reach it indirectly, through patterns, triggers and projections.

The Jungian frame of the shadow: to build a personality that earns love and belonging, we split off the traits our family and culture could not accept, and lock them in a vault. Since the shadow is designed to stay hidden, we approach it indirectly through its three mechanisms — patterns, triggers and projections. The premise: the shadow is not negative; the vault of the shadow holds our most powerful suppressed energies, and integration ("I don't want to be a good person, I want to be a complete person" — Jung) is the aim.

### Psychedelic Studies, Harm Reduction & Integration
> A study track, not a practice of the school: history, set and setting, ethics, and the craft of integration.

A study track (not a practice of the school) covering the history and therapeutic renaissance of psychedelics — from Grof's LSD research to current clinical work — together with set and setting, harm reduction principles, facilitation ethics, and the crucial craft of integration: somatic, narrative, creative and relational tools for turning an expanded state into lasting change. Holotropic Breathwork is presented as the non-pharmacological path through the same territory.

### The Hero's Journey & the Evolution of Consciousness
> Campbell’s monomyth as the deep structure of transformation, repeating at every scale of a life.

Campbell's monomyth as the deep structure of transformation — the call, the crisis, the descent, the return — repeating fractally at every scale of a life. Alongside it, the study of humanity's evolving "tools of evolution": myths, rituals, tarot, philosophy and science as successive maps for navigating what is real.

### The Body–Mind Foundations
> Damasio, Porges and van der Kolk — why breath, body and safe relationship are the primary levers of change.

The scientific ground of the experiential work: Damasio's homeostasis and the theory of feelings as the mind's report on the state of life; Porges' Polyvagal Theory, co-regulation and the vagal brake; van der Kolk's work on how the body keeps the score of trauma. Together they explain *why* breath, body and safe relationship are the primary levers of change.
